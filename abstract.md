# Risk-Aware and Cost-Effective Validation of Manufacturing Knowledge Across Changing Production Systems

## Abstract

Manufacturing systems accumulate valuable operational knowledge about machine performance, production bottlenecks, changeover times, and process reliability. However, equipment upgrades, production reconfigurations, and facility relocation can make previously acquired knowledge unreliable. Revalidating every knowledge item is costly, while reusing incorrect knowledge can lead to poor operational decisions. This creates an important decision problem: which knowledge can still be trusted, and which items require verification before reuse?

In this work, we propose a framework for validating manufacturing knowledge after production system changes. The framework integrates four components: (1) change characterization to identify differences between source and target production environments; (2) a knowledge graph linking knowledge items to the operating conditions they depend on; (3) a Bayesian trust model that estimates and updates the probability that each knowledge item remains valid as new evidence becomes available; and (4) a decision agent that selects verification activities based on expected value of information, decision risk, and time and budget constraints.

The framework is evaluated using discrete event simulation under two scenarios: equipment replacement or upgrades and relocation of production assets to a new facility. Performance is compared with full revalidation, fixed duration monitoring, and validation methods without evidence sharing. Evaluation measures include total decision and verification cost, incorrect knowledge reuse, and decision time. Through the proposed framework we aim to reduce verification costs while supporting reliable knowledge reuse and operational decision making.

## Keywords

Manufacturing Knowledge Transfer; Knowledge Graphs; Bayesian Networks; Value of Information; Risk-Aware Decision-Making; Discrete-Event Simulation
